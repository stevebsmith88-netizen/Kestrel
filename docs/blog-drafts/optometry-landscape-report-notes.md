# Canadian Optometry Landscape Report: notes for blog posts

Source: "The Canadian Optometry Landscape Report", 2026 Canadian New ODs Survey, by Dr. Wynn Nguyen, O.D., sponsored by OSI Group. 460 optometrists answered. 3 parts (Steve sent them on 18th November 2026 as public sources). Page numbers below are the slide numbers in each PDF.

Read so far: Part 1 pages 1 to 6 and 18 to 35; Part 2 all; Part 3 pages 1 to 6 and 28 to 62. **Not read yet:** the student debt chapter in full, ownership routes (Part 3 pages 7 to 27), mobility, succession and transition, and the chapter on associations and buying groups (Part 3 pages 63 to 92). Read the buying groups chapter before using it: any group named must be treated like every other group, from public information only, alphabetical (see CLAUDE.md, Eye Recommend rule).

## How to cite it

- Say "a 2026 survey of 460 Canadian optometrists" and name the report and its author, with a link. Say "typical" and "median", never "average" or "national average".
- It is a self selected survey, not a census. 57% graduated in 2013 or later and 312 of 460 trained at Waterloo (Part 1, pages 5 and 6). Say "respondents".
- Income is self reported take home before tax (Part 2, page 4), not a pay offer. Keep it apart from Kestrel's pay benchmark and Job Bank figures, and label it.
- Skip any cell with fewer than 10 answers. Examples: Manitoba (8), Quebec (6), Kitchener-Waterloo (6), Victoria (3).
- Kestrel does not cover Quebec, so leave Quebec out.
- Quote a figure or two with credit. Do not copy charts or tables.

## Working year (Part 1, pages 21 to 35)

- Median respondent: 4 days a week, 200 days a year, 33 patient hours a week, 15 patients a day (12 full exams), 24 vacation days.
- Associates work more than owners: 4.5 days against 4.0 a week, and 44% of associates work 5 or more days against 22% of owners.
- Patients a day by setting (all visits, full exams): private practice 15 and 11; corporate doctor-led 17.5 and 13; OMD-OD practice 20 and 12.5; side-by-side independent 14 and 10 (page 29). **This does not support the "20 to 35 a day in corporate" figure from the Reddit summary. Do not use that figure.**

## Income (Part 2)

- Median take home $175,000 a year (mean $191,420), $841 a day (pages 4 and 7). Owners $200,000, associates $168,310 (pages 5 and 6).
- Ontario median $169,000 (n 181), Toronto $150,000, Alberta $163,445, BC $180,000, Saskatchewan $230,000 (n 19) (pages 16 to 20).
- By community size, annual median: rural $190,000 (n 33), small city $185,000, medium $169,000, large $169,000, metropolitan $160,000. Per day worked: rural $993, small city $962, metropolitan $800 (pages 21 and 22).
- Income peaks at a 4.5 day week ($190,000), and income per day falls as the week gets longer (page 28).

## How optometrists are paid (Part 2, pages 32 to 37; Part 3, pages 42 to 50)

- 9 in 10 associates are paid a percentage of production. Median 23%, and about 3 in 4 sit between 20% and 25%.
- 32% of associates receive a guaranteed minimum, median $600 a day, 3 in 4 of them daily. Owners say the same ($600). 33% of owners offer one.
- Share with a guarantee by province: BC 63%, Alberta 62%, Ontario 21%, Saskatchewan 8% (page 49).
- The survey did not ask whether the percentage covers services, products or both (Part 2, page 35). This is the same gap Kestrel's pay rules close by asking for expected earnings in dollars.

## Recruiting and keeping associates (Part 3, pages 39 to 62)

- 1 in 5 owners surveyed is struggling to recruit. 63% of owners without an associate report recruiting challenges. 71% of those say too few candidates in their region (page 41).
- By region, owners struggling to recruit: Prairies 40%, Ontario 11% (page 56). Small sample, so say "directional".
- Time to hire: 35% under 6 months, 63% within a year, 15% over 2 years (page 55).
- 70% of owners with associates have never given a percentage raise. 55% of associates have never asked. Of those who asked, 68% got one (pages 59 and 60).
- Associates who asked and were refused (n 20): 80% name a reason to leave (page 61). Directional only.
- Reasons to leave: income lower than expected 26%, clinic culture 17%, start own practice 14%, not planning to leave 25% (page 62).
- What matters when moving clinic: work and life balance 81%, income 79%, clinic culture 61%, close to family 59%, guaranteed minimum 15% (page 58).

## Why optometrists chose their practice (Part 3, pages 31 to 38)

- Ranked first: autonomy 39%, income 30%, clinic culture 23%. Specialty and mentorship rank last for 43% each. **So mentorship is not the main pull.** Do not lead with it.
- Newer grads lean to income: 45% of 2021 to 2025 grads rank income first, against 19% before 2000.
- Income is the top reason in corporate settings (51% to 54%), autonomy in private practice (43%).

## Ownership (Part 3, pages 4 to 6 and 28 to 29)

- About half of respondents who started in private practice, OMD-OD or side-by-side corporate are owners today, against 25% to 31% of those who started in corporate or franchise settings.
- 45% of associates want to own in the next 10 years. The biggest barrier is financial risk (40% rank it first), then work and life balance (35%).
