## Global: Clinical Optometry, 26th August 2024

Gammoh Y, Morjaria P, Block SS, Massie J, et al. "2023 Global Survey of Optometry: Defining Variations of Practice, Regulation and Human Resources Between Countries", Clin Optom (Auckl) 2024;16:211 to 220, doi 10.2147/OPTO.S481096, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11363951/

- Run January to December 2023 with the World Council of Optometry's country members. 39 of 40 invited countries took part. Non random, purposive sampling, 1 key informant per country nominated by the national association, a questionnaire adapted from ECOO's, then a video interview to check answers.
- Mean of country figures: 45% men, 55% women. High income countries (n=12): average 63% women. Sub-Saharan Africa (n=8): "predominantly male (average 62%)" in the results and "only 37%" women in the discussion (the 1 point difference is the paper's own, so the post says 37%).
- Table 1 gives each country's split in steps of 5%. Examples used: Philippines 80% women, Palestine 80%, Bangladesh 25%, Uganda 20%. The discussion text says Kenya 20% but the table says 15%, so the post does not quote Kenya.
- **Do not rank countries from it.** Canada and the United States both show 70% women, against about 59 to 63% (Canada) and about 49% (United States) from national counts. The post says so.

## National counts

- **United States:** Data USA, women 48.6% of nearly 43,000 practising optometrists in 2022, and Women in Optometry (WIO) school figures, via Eyes on Eyecare, "Future of optometry is increasingly women, report finds", 17th October 2024, https://glance.eyesoneyecare.com/stories/2024-10-17/future-of-optometry-is-increasingly-women-report-finds/ . Also from it: women 2.1% (368) of active ODs in 1968; 19% of school entrants in 1979; 54% in 1999 to 2000; 68% in 2017 to 2018; 71% of more than 2,000 first year students at 26 North American schools (including Puerto Rico and Canada) for the class of 2028; fourth year in a row at 70% or more; 40% of practising ODs in 2012 and 48% in 2023. Its pay figures are left out on purpose (not adjusted for age, years or ownership).
- **United States (workforce survey):** Review of Optometry on the 2017 National Optometry Workforce Survey (about 4,000 ODs, Optometry and Vision Science), https://www.reviewofoptometry.com/article/optometric-workforce-survey-projects-modest-growth-more-employed-ods : AOA database 45% women in 2017 (20,249) and 49% by January 2020 (23,367); hours 37.5 (women) against 38.9 (men), not significant; patients an hour 1.97 against 2; 77% of women and 78% of men prefer the same or fewer patient hours; women tended to be employed, male respondents older and more often owners.
- **England:** College of Optometrists, Acuity, 27th February 2020, "Is there a gender gulf in optometry?", https://www.college-optometrists.org/acuity/all-issues/acuity-digital/is-there-a-gender-gulf-in-optometry : 51.2% of optometrists and ophthalmic medical practitioners were men in 2008, women 54.9% in 2019 (NHS Digital), and 70% of trainees in 2018 to 2019 were women. Counts both optometrists and ophthalmic medical practitioners.
- **Australia:** Department of Health, "Optometrists 2019" factsheet (NHWDS 2015 to 2019), https://hwd.health.gov.au/resources/publications/factsheet-alld-optometrists-2019.pdf : 54.9% female.
- **Canada:** see `women-in-eye-care-research.md` (Statistics Canada table 98-10-0449-01, https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=9810044901 , and the CAO 2025 report).
