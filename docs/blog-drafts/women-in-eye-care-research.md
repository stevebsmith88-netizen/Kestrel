# Women in eye care: census research (14th November 2026)

Source: Statistics Canada, 2021 Census of Population, Table 98-10-0449-01 (occupation unit group by labour force status, education, age and gender), total labour force, all education levels. Pulled through Statistics Canada's web data service on 14th November 2026. Statistics Canada rounds census counts to the nearest 5, so small numbers jump about: do not quote shares for Prince Edward Island or Newfoundland and Labrador optometrists. Credit: "Source: Statistics Canada, 2021 Census". Kestrel publishes no Quebec content, so the post uses Canada totals and other provinces only.

Gender in the census is "Men+" and "Women+" (Statistics Canada adds the small non binary count into these 2 groups to protect privacy). Say so in the post.

## The story: a generational shift

| Age | Optometrists: women | Opticians: women |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 25 to 34 | 69% (1,215 of 1,765) | 79% (1,975 of 2,495) |
| 35 to 44 | 66% | 78% |
| 45 to 54 | 56% | 71% |
| 55 to 64 | 50% | 56% |
| 65 and over | 14% (60 of 420) | 33% (190 of 570) |

Among optometrists 65 and over, about 1 in 7 is a woman. Among those 25 to 34, more than 2 in 3. As the older group retires, a growing share of practice owners and senior opticians will be women.

## Newer headcount (for context)

Statistics Canada's "Vision health month" page says 6,400 optometrists and 11,700 opticians worked in Canada in 2023 (a different measure from the census, so do not mix the 2 in 1 sentence). Gender split for 2023 is not given there, so the census stays the source for the women and men figures.

## Canadian Association of Optometrists, "Optometry in Canada: Optometric Leaders' Forum 2025 Report"

Shared by Steve as a PDF on 14th November 2026. Gathered by the CAO from the provincial associations and colleges, both schools and student leaders. Steve confirmed on 14th November 2026 that it is a public PDF on opto.ca. Link to it there when quoting, and cite it as the CAO's report, not as Kestrel's numbers.

- **Gender:** women 3,974, men 2,336, other or not specified 3 (63% women), excluding British Columbia, which did not report. Agrees with the census (59% in 2021) and suggests the share is still rising.
- **By province (women and men):** Alberta 519 and 428; Manitoba 106 and 94; New Brunswick 86 and 51; Newfoundland and Labrador 34 and 23; Nova Scotia 103 and 59; Ontario 1,829 and 1,162; Prince Edward Island 16 and 8; Saskatchewan 114 and 86. (Quebec is in the report but Kestrel publishes no Quebec content.)
- **Age, Canada:** the largest groups are 30 to 34 (1,195) and 35 to 39 (1,222). 548 are 65 or over (312 aged 65 to 69, 151 aged 70 to 74, 85 aged 75 and over).
- **Optometrists by province:** Ontario 2,993; British Columbia 981; Alberta 947; Manitoba 200; Saskatchewan 200; Nova Scotia 162; New Brunswick 137; Newfoundland and Labrador 57; Prince Edward Island 24. Per 100,000 people: Alberta 22.2, Ontario 21, British Columbia 19.6, New Brunswick and Saskatchewan 17.7, Nova Scotia 16.7, Prince Edward Island 15.6, Manitoba 14.9, Newfoundland and Labrador 11.2.
- **Owners and associates:** Ontario 1,080 owners or decision makers, 1,636 associates, contractors or employees; Saskatchewan 139 and 54; Manitoba 114 and 81; New Brunswick 53 and 80. Most young optometrists work as associates first: relevant to the "path to ownership" angle.
- **Students:** 137 in the most recent graduating class (94 Waterloo, 43 Montréal), plus 28 in the internationally trained programs (IOBP at Montréal, ASOPP at Waterloo).
- **What students say:** a "saturated job market" in cities (Canada has one of the highest numbers of optometrists per person in the world), while every province reports a shortage outside urban centres. Students choose where to work on growth, location, salary, work and life balance, scope and working with other optometrists.
- **Top public questions to the colleges and associations:** cost (exams, glasses, billing, extra tests, coverage, refunds), then finding an optometrist.

## Angles

1. **Data post (first):** "Who works in eye care in Canada": the generational shift, the provincial differences (Ontario opticians are much more evenly split than the Prairies), and what it means for hiring: flexible hours, return after leave, the path from optician to owner.
2. **Men in eye care, inside the data post:** men are about 1 in 5 younger opticians and a smaller share of assisting roles. Practical angle for clinics: if your ads and your team page only ever show 1 kind of person, you may be narrowing the field. Ties to "hire for attitude".
3. **Women in Eye Care series, around International Women's Day (Monday 8th March 2027):** short profiles of real women with their permission, found outside Eye Recommend contacts.
