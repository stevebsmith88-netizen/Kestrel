**Title:** Eye care hiring in Canada: what to watch in 2027

**Description:** The rules, numbers and trends likely to shape hiring in Canadian eye care clinics in 2027, for clinic owners and for people looking for their next role.

**Body:**

Nobody can tell you exactly what 2027 holds for eye care hiring, and we will not pretend to. What we can do is point to the things already in motion, the ones with a date on them or a public source behind them, and say why each matters to a clinic or to someone looking for work. The last section is our own view, and it is marked as such.

## More people needing eye care

Canada's population is getting older, and the need for eye exams and treatment rises with age [check: cite the Statistics Canada figure for the share of Canadians aged 65 and over]. More patients over 65 means more exams, more follow up and more chair time, which lands on optometrists, opticians and the support team together. It is a slow trend, so it rarely makes headlines, but it is the reason most clinics we hear from feel busier each year.

## Job Bank's outlook

The federal government's Job Bank rates job prospects by occupation and province for 3 years at a time. Its current outlook, for 2025 to 2027, rates prospects for optometrists as very good in British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan, with retirements and growth given as the reasons [check the current ratings for every province, and for opticians]. A "very good" rating means employers in that province are likely to find hiring harder, and candidates are likely to have more choice. If you are hiring in one of those provinces, plan for a longer search. If you are looking, it is a good year to ask for what you want.

## Pay ranges in job ads

Since 1st January 2026, Ontario employers with 25 or more employees have to include a pay range in a publicly advertised job, along with a few other things. British Columbia already requires a pay range in public job postings [check the date it started and who it covers]. [Check whether any other province has passed or announced a similar rule for 2027, and name it with its date if so.]

Even where the rules do not apply, pay ranges are becoming what people expect to see. A job ad without one now reads as if the clinic has something to hide, even when it does not. Every job on Kestrel shows its pay for that reason. Our [job ad checklist](/blog/eye-care-job-ad-checklist) covers what to include, and our [pay table](/resources#pay) shows typical pay by province from Job Bank.

## Saying whether you use AI to screen applicants

Ontario's rules also ask covered employers to say in a public job posting whether they use artificial intelligence to screen, assess or select applicants. Few clinics do, but many recruitment tools now include some automatic sorting, and a clinic using one may not think of it as AI. If you post through a large job site or an applicant tracking system, it is worth asking the provider what it does with applications before you answer.

## What else Ontario's rules changed

Covered Ontario employers cannot ask for "Canadian experience" in a public job posting, have to say whether the posting is for an existing vacancy, and have to let interviewed applicants know whether a decision has been made [check: the 45 day rule and record keeping period]. Our [plain guide to Ontario's rules](/resources/ontario-job-ads) has the detail. Smaller clinics are not covered, but candidates will start to expect the same courtesy everywhere.

## If you are planning for 2027

For clinics, the practical list is short. Put a pay range on every job ad whether or not you are covered by a rule. Answer every applicant you interview. Start the search for a role before the person leaving has gone, because in the provinces where the outlook is strongest, the search will take longer. Our guide to [writing an optometric assistant job ad](/blog/optometric-assistant-job-ad) is a good place to start.

For job seekers, the strongest position is a current profile and an alert for your role and province, so you hear about the right job in the week it appears. If you are thinking about a move between provinces, the outlook ratings above are worth a look before you decide.
