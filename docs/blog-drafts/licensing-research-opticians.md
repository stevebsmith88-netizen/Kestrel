# Optician licensing research (for the 8th December 2026 guide)

Read on the regulators' own websites on 13th November 2026. Every line below says where it came from. Facts only, in Kestrel's words where possible. Check again on the day the guide is written.

## National (NACOR)

Source: nacor.ca/national-examinations/ and nacor.ca/how-to-become-an-optician/

- The National Alliance of Canadian Optician Regulators (NACOR) runs the national exams for opticians on behalf of the provincial regulators. They are used in every province except Quebec.
- There are 2 national exams: 1 for eyeglass dispensing and 1 for contact lens dispensing. Ontario's college names them "Optical Sciences 1, Eyeglass Examination" and "Optical Sciences 2, Advanced Practice Contact Lens Examination".
- Both exams were updated from 1st March 2025. NACOR says the competencies tested did not change.
- 2 ways in: graduate from an accredited Canadian opticianry program, or complete Prior Learning Assessment and Recognition (PLAR). Both routes must pass the national exam(s) to register in their province. A province may add its own requirements.
- Applicants do not have to wait for graduation confirmation, or for the provincial regulator, before applying for the exam. Applications close at a deadline. Applications are made at opticianry.ca/nacor/exams.
- NACOR emails exam details (location, start time) about 2 weeks before the exam.
- PLAR was updated in March 2026, and PLAR fees rose from 1st April 2025. NACOR says some of its pages are still being updated.
- Since 1st October 2019, opticianry programs are accredited by Accreditation Canada.

## Ontario: College of Opticians of Ontario

Source: collegeofopticians.ca, applicant pages

- To sit the national exams you first register as a **Registered Intern Optician**, if you graduated from an approved program in the 3 years before applying (or completed training the Registration Committee required).
- The intern certificate lasts 3 years, or until you pass the national exams, whichever comes first. An extension needs the Registration Committee.
- An intern may dispense only under the supervision of a registered optician, optometrist or physician, and must complete any practicum hours their program did not cover.
- The application needs a recent passport photo, a letter confirming the program was completed, practicum evidence, notarized proof of name and of eligibility to work, and letters of standing from any other regulator. PLAR applicants also show English or French proficiency.
- Applications are online only since 1st December 2024, except for accommodation or French language applicants.
- Separate routes exist for unaccredited education and labour mobility (opticians registered in another province).

## Alberta: College of Opticians of Alberta

Source: albertaopticians.ca, applicants and provisional opticians pages

- You must register with the College to practise as an optician or contact lens practitioner in Alberta.
- Graduates register as a **Provisional Optician** to be eligible to write the NACOR exam and to work. Steps: apply for provisional registration, work under supervision, pass the NACOR exam(s), complete the final application.
- A provisional optician is supervised by a licensed optician, optometrist or ophthalmologist.
- The provisional application includes a criminal record check, a declaration of good character, professional liability insurance, supervisor details, the fee and proof of education (or PLAR results).
- Graduates of non-accredited programs and international candidates complete PLAR, plus English language requirements.
- Other routes listed: courtesy (temporary) permit, opticians from out of province, non-accredited education, international candidates.

## Manitoba: Opticians of Manitoba

Source: opticiansofmanitoba.ca, application and licensure page

- By law, everyone practising as an optician or contact lens optician in Manitoba must be registered and licensed with the Opticians of Manitoba.
- Licence categories: Licensed Optician (eyeglasses and subnormal vision devices, on a prescription from an optometrist, ophthalmologist or medical doctor), Contact Lens Licensed Optician (contact lenses as well), Inactive or Non-Practising, and Student Optician.
- Applicants must meet the education requirements and pass the exam(s) for eyeglass dispensing and/or contact lens fitting, and keep up continuing education ("Maintenance of Competency").
- Separate pages: student registration, international candidates.
