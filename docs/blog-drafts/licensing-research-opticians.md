# Optician licensing research (for the 8th December 2026 guide)

Read on the regulators' own websites on 13th November 2026. Every line below says where it came from. Facts only, in Kestrel's words where possible. Check again on the day the guide is written.

## National (NACOR)

Source: nacor.ca/national-examinations/ and nacor.ca/how-to-become-an-optician/

- The National Alliance of Canadian Optician Regulators (NACOR) runs the national exams for opticians on behalf of the provincial regulators. They are used in every province except Quebec.
- There are 2 national exams: 1 for eyeglass dispensing and 1 for contact lens dispensing. Ontario's college names them "Optical Sciences 1, Eyeglass Examination" and "Optical Sciences 2, Advanced Practice Contact Lens Examination".
- Both exams were updated from 1st March 2025. NACOR says the competencies tested did not change.
- 2 ways in: graduate from an accredited Canadian opticianry program, or complete Prior Learning Assessment and Recognition (PLAR). Both routes must pass the national exam(s) to register in their province. A province may add its own requirements.
- Applicants do not have to wait for graduation confirmation, or for the provincial regulator, before applying for the exam. Applications close at a deadline. Applications are made at opticianry.ca/nacor/exams.
- NACOR emails exam details (location, start time) about 2 weeks before the exam.
- PLAR was updated in March 2026, and PLAR fees rose from 1st April 2025. NACOR says some of its pages are still being updated.
- Since 1st October 2019, opticianry programs are accredited by Accreditation Canada.

## Ontario: College of Opticians of Ontario

Source: collegeofopticians.ca, applicant pages

- To sit the national exams you first register as a **Registered Intern Optician**, if you graduated from an approved program in the 3 years before applying (or completed training the Registration Committee required).
- The intern certificate lasts 3 years, or until you pass the national exams, whichever comes first. An extension needs the Registration Committee.
- An intern may dispense only under the supervision of a registered optician, optometrist or physician, and must complete any practicum hours their program did not cover.
- The application needs a recent passport photo, a letter confirming the program was completed, practicum evidence, notarized proof of name and of eligibility to work, and letters of standing from any other regulator. PLAR applicants also show English or French proficiency.
- Applications are online only since 1st December 2024, except for accommodation or French language applicants.
- Separate routes exist for unaccredited education and labour mobility (opticians registered in another province).

## Alberta: College of Opticians of Alberta

Source: albertaopticians.ca, applicants and provisional opticians pages

- You must register with the College to practise as an optician or contact lens practitioner in Alberta.
- Graduates register as a **Provisional Optician** to be eligible to write the NACOR exam and to work. Steps: apply for provisional registration, work under supervision, pass the NACOR exam(s), complete the final application.
- A provisional optician is supervised by a licensed optician, optometrist or ophthalmologist.
- The provisional application includes a criminal record check, a declaration of good character, professional liability insurance, supervisor details, the fee and proof of education (or PLAR results).
- Graduates of non-accredited programs and international candidates complete PLAR, plus English language requirements.
- Other routes listed: courtesy (temporary) permit, opticians from out of province, non-accredited education, international candidates.

## Manitoba: Opticians of Manitoba

Source: opticiansofmanitoba.ca, application and licensure page

- By law, everyone practising as an optician or contact lens optician in Manitoba must be registered and licensed with the Opticians of Manitoba.
- Licence categories: Licensed Optician (eyeglasses and subnormal vision devices, on a prescription from an optometrist, ophthalmologist or medical doctor), Contact Lens Licensed Optician (contact lenses as well), Inactive or Non-Practising, and Student Optician.
- Applicants must meet the education requirements and pass the exam(s) for eyeglass dispensing and/or contact lens fitting, and keep up continuing education ("Maintenance of Competency").
- Separate pages: student registration, international candidates.

## British Columbia: College of Health and Care Professionals of BC

Source: chcpbc.org, the opticianry application steps, pasted by Steve on 14th November 2026 (the site refuses automated visits)

- 13 steps. First an equivalency determination, unless the program is a Recognized Education Program in Schedule 5 of the college's bylaws. Nothing else can start until that is confirmed.
- 2 classes: **Full** licensure needs both national exams (Optical Sciences 1 and 2) passed. **Provisional** licensure allows supervised practice while waiting, if you are registered for the next sittings of both exams. A provisional licensee works only under a Full licensee the college has approved (Supervision Agreement).
- English proficiency: not needed if the recognized program was in English within the last 5 years; otherwise an approved test, sent directly by the testing agency.
- Official transcripts sent by the school. A program not on the recognized list also needs a credential evaluation (ICAS, ICES, IQAS or WES).
- Verification from every regulator you have ever been licensed with, sent by them within 60 days before you apply.
- Professional liability insurance of at least $2 million per claim (an employer's policy may count).
- 2 notarized identity documents, at least 1 from the primary list.
- Online application in the Licensee Portal (Alinity), with a criminal record check, the application fee, then the annual fee. The licence year runs 1st April to 31st March, prorated from 1st October.

## Saskatchewan: Saskatchewan College of Opticians

Source: the college's accredited education page, pasted by Steve on 14th November 2026

- Bylaws require graduation from an accredited program (or one the council deems equivalent). A Canadian high school diploma or assessed equivalent is needed to start.
- You register with the college as a **student optician**. After the eyeglass program, you may sit the NACOR exam, then apply to register. Once licensed, an optician may dispense eyeglasses in Saskatchewan.
- NAIT's distance program needs you to be working in the industry, with a qualified supervisor licensed in Saskatchewan (an optician, contact lens optician, optometrist or ophthalmologist).
- The college lists the accredited schools with an accreditation status and expiry. Several expiry dates shown there have passed (for example Douglas College 30th September 2025, NAIT 30th September 2024), so the guide should not quote accreditation status: point readers to Accreditation Canada for the current list. That list also includes the Ontario College of Optics (London, "Registered").

## New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, Newfoundland and Labrador, Prince Edward Island (read 14th November 2026)

- **New Brunswick** (the province's "Opticians: pathway to registration for internationally educated professionals", March 2026, nbjobs.ca): registration with the Opticians Association of New Brunswick is required to use the title. Minimum education is an opticianry diploma from a program accredited by Accreditation Canada; others go through NACOR's PLAR (competency gap analysis, then up to 18 bridging modules within 1 year), then the national exam. Licence: request a package by email from the association; usually approved within 1 day to 1 week. Cost for an internationally educated optician: about $2,200 (eyeglasses only, no bridging) to about $5,360 (eyeglasses and contact lenses, all bridging), as of February 2026; usually 1 year or longer.
- **Nova Scotia** (nscdo.org, student registration page): register as a student optician before being accepted into a training program; you must already work in an optical dispensary with a preceptor who is a registered optician or optometrist. You must be a fully licensed optician before registering as a student contact lens fitter. Other routes: new graduate, non accredited and international, out of province transfer. Office: Spring Garden Road, Halifax, registrar@nscdo.org.
- **Newfoundland and Labrador** (Dispensing Opticians Board annual report 2021 to 2022, gov.nl.ca): regulated since 1982; 114 licensed opticians at the time; about 20 senior opticians expected to retire within 3 years. International applicants: PLAR, any bridging the board sets, then the national exam or an accredited program. Signatory to mutual recognition agreements with 9 other provinces. Protected titles: Optician, Licensed Optician, Registered Optician, Certified Contact Lens Fitter, Registered Contact Lens Fitter, Contact Lens Fitter.
- **Prince Edward Island**: Job Bank confirms the regulator is the Prince Edward Island Board of Dispensing Opticians. No website found; the guide tells readers to contact the board.
