**Title:** Eye care in Canada by the numbers

**Description:** How many optometrists, opticians and practices there are in Canada, how much hiring happens in a year, and what the numbers mean if you are hiring or looking.

**Body:**

Eye care can feel like a small world. People know each other across clinics, reps cover whole regions, and a good optician changing jobs is often news by the end of the week. The numbers say something a little different. Eye care in Canada is a large field, spread across a very large country, and that shapes how hiring works.

## The people

Statistics Canada counted about 6,400 optometrists and about 11,700 opticians working in Canada in 2023 [check]. Counts of optometrists depend on who is counting and how. Other sources put the number anywhere from about 6,000 to just over 7,100, depending on the year and whether they count licences or people in work [check]. However you count, there are close to 2 opticians for every optometrist.

Optometric assistants, technicians and front desk staff are harder to count. The official figures group them with other health support jobs, so there is no national number for eye care alone. Our own working assumption is 1.5 to 2.5 for every optometrist, which would mean somewhere between 10,000 and 16,000 people. That is an estimate, and we would be glad to hear from anyone with a better one. Add practice managers, and eye care employs somewhere around 31,000 to 39,000 people in Canada.

## The practices

Industry directories list more than 4,000 optometry practices across the country, and one puts the figure closer to 7,000, depending on what it counts as a separate business [check]. On top of that are optical stores that do not have an optometrist on site. Opticians work in both: Job Bank says about 3 in 4 work in retail settings and about 1 in 4 in health care settings [check].

## The hiring

By our own estimate, more than 2,000 eye care roles are advertised in Canada every year. We say "more than" because it is a floor, and our working range runs much higher. No one publishes a national count, so treat it as an estimate.

The more useful number is the small one. Most practices hire only once or twice a year. A clinic that hires that rarely writes each job ad more or less from scratch, has no recent sense of what the role pays elsewhere, and is often short a person while it does it. Job seekers see the same thing from the other side: in a given month there may be only a handful of openings for their role within driving distance, and then several at once.

Job Bank's outlook for 2025 to 2027 rates job prospects for optometrists as very good in British Columbia, Alberta and Saskatchewan, with retirements and growth given as the reasons, and it notes a shortage of opticians in parts of British Columbia [check the current wording and years].

## What it means if you are hiring

When few people are looking at any one time, the details in your ad decide who applies. A clear pay range, the hours, and what a normal day looks like do more than a long list of duties. ## What it means if you are looking

A field this size rewards being findable. Many roles are filled through people who already know you, so it is worth keeping in touch with former colleagues and reps. Set a job alert for your role and province, so you hear about the handful of openings near you when they appear. Our guide to [the roles in eye care](/blog/working-in-eye-care) explains who does what if you are deciding where to aim.
