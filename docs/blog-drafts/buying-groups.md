## The draft

**Title:** How to choose an optical buying group

**Description:** What a buying group does for an independent eye care practice, the questions worth asking before you join, and a short look at the main groups in Canada.

A buying group lets independent practices buy together. The big idea is simple: 1 practice buying frames and lenses has little weight with a supplier, and a few hundred practices buying together have more. What you get beyond a discount varies a lot from group to group, and so does what you give up.

Start with what you actually buy. If most of your spend is with 3 or 4 suppliers, check that those suppliers are in the group and what the discount is on your real orders, not on the price list. Ask whether you can still buy from a supplier outside the group, and whether any rebate depends on how much you buy from the group's preferred names.

Then ask about the rest of the package. Some groups offer consolidated billing, so 1 invoice replaces a dozen. Some offer practice software, staff training, marketing, financing for patients, or help recruiting. Each is worth something only if you would otherwise pay for it or do without it. Add up the ones you would use and compare that with the fee, if there is one.

Ownership matters more than most practices expect. Ask who controls the group: its members, a company, or a larger retail business. Ask what happens to your terms if ownership changes. Ask how long the agreement runs, how you leave, and whether there is a notice period or a fee. Then ask 2 or 3 current members, not the ones the group suggests, what they would change.

Which professional the group is built around is worth a question too. Some groups were started by optometrists and some serve opticians and optical shops, and a few run a separate group for each.

## A short look at the main groups

Each of these describes itself on its own site, and each will have changed since this was written. Check the details with the group.

- **Eye Recommend** is a co-operative of independent optometrists, founded in 1985 according to published profiles. It says members get buying power, consolidated billing, education and business support, and it runs a second group, Eye Care Collective, for optical professionals. Its size is reported in different ways in different places (over 500 clinics in one source, over 1,300 optometrists in another), so ask the group for a current number.
- **OSI Group (Optometric Services Inc.)** says it has over 1,800 members and discounts with more than 130 suppliers, along with private label products, practice software, staff training and recruiting support, and no membership fees.
- **The Optical Group** was founded in 1988 and says it offers group purchasing, consolidated billing, digital marketing, education and private labels. Trade press has reported changes in its ownership (a 2021 sale to New Look Vision Group, and a 2026 investment in the Academy of Ophthalmic Education), so ask who owns it today.

A note on where this comes from: Steve Smith, who founded Kestrel, works at Eye Recommend. This post is written to help you ask better questions of any group, including that one.
