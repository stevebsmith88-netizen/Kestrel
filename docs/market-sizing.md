## Sensitivity: how often clinics hire

Steve's experience is that many clinics recruit far more often than once or twice a year. That fits the high turnover typical of optical retail and clinic support roles, and public data cannot confirm or deny it. So here is the same model with higher turnover. The public home page deliberately uses only the cautious floor.

| Case | Turnover and growth a year | Hires a year | Roles advertised (60% to 80%) | Per practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cautious (used on the home page) | 12% to 22% | 3,700 to 8,600 | 2,200 to 6,900 | 0.3 to 1.6 |
| Higher turnover (Steve's experience) | 25% to 40% | 7,800 to 15,600 | 4,700 to 12,500 | 0.7 to 2.9 |

At the higher turnover, the plan's roughly 800 paid posts a year is about 6% to 17% of all advertised roles, not 20% or more. That is far easier to reach, and means the revenue plan is more credible than the cautious case suggests. Which case is right is the most important unknown in the plan. Steve's weekly counts, and Kestrel's own traffic in its first months, will show it.

## A note on member clinics

Several hundred of the independent clinics in Canada are members of Steve's employer. Using no employer data and no member contact means they are out of reach unless an arrangement is agreed. If they are around a sixth to a third of independent practices (about 2,000 to 4,000, above), the rest of the market is still large, but a members benefit arrangement would add a lot. This depends on the disclosure and the lawyer (pack, Part A). The figure is deliberately kept out of this repository.

## What this means for the plan (coaching view)

The revenue plan in `docs/PLAN.md` needs about **47 clinic passes and 550 single posts**.

- **Clinics:** 550 single posts is about 420 paying clinics, which is roughly 6% to 10% of all practices, or 10% to 20% of independent ones. That is ambitious but not absurd for a well run niche board by year 2.
- **Roles:** 550 single posts plus 47 passes is about 800 paid posts a year if passes average about 5 posts each. Against roughly 4,000 advertised roles, that is about **20%** of the national volume, and about 12% to 36% across the range. This is the stretch. It is a bigger share than the clinic count suggests, because most practices only hire 1 or 2 times a year.
- **Who buys a pass:** at about 1 role a practice a year, a $199 a month pass pays off only for multi-location groups, larger independents, suppliers hiring reps, and clinics in a hiring wave. Expect fewer passes than the plan assumes, and test the pass with that audience first.
- **Where the volume is:** chains and large groups post the most roles. Their ads usually run on their own careers sites, which is why seeding from clinics' own careers pages matters, and why the supplier and field role categories are worth keeping in the product.
- **Do not trust the denominator yet.** Roles a year could be half or double the central case. A month of weekly counts of eye care jobs on public job sites (counts only, by hand) will narrow it, and so will the first months of real traffic.

## How to firm these numbers up (all public, free)

1. Statistics Canada: Census or Labour Force Survey tables for NOC 31111 (optometrists), 32100 (opticians) and 33109 (assistants), and the Job Vacancy and Wage Survey table 14-10-0398-01.
2. Canadian Association of Optometrists, "Optometry in Canada" 2025 and 2026 reports: practice types and provincial counts. info@opto.ca takes data requests.
3. Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada, Canadian Industry Statistics, for NAICS 621320 and optical goods retailers, by employment size.
4. Provincial college registers show counts only. Reading a count is fine, but copying names or addresses is not allowed under the project rules.
5. Use no data from your employer, its members or its internal reports, so the separation from your job stays clean.
