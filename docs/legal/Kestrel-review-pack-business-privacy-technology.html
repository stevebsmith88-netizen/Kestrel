Part 1. The business in one page

What it is. Kestrel Eye Care Jobs (eyecarejobs.ca) is a self serve job board for Canadian eye care: optometrists, opticians, optometric assistants and technicians, practice managers, patient care teams and, later, supplier representatives and locums. Independent and corporate practices post side by side. Candidates search free; clinics pay to post.

Where it stands. Not launched. A "coming soon" page with a waitlist is public. Accounts, job posting, applications, an admin review queue and an AI job ad writer are built and tested, but only with my own test accounts. There are no real users, no payments and no outreach to clinics. Clinic sign up is by invitation only until launch. Target: a free launch in January 2027 at the earliest, only after the company, policy and other legal matters in this pack are settled. A blog is being written to build search ranking over time.

The people. I am the only founder and the only person with access. I am building Kestrel alongside other work. I am not a developer: the software is written by an AI coding assistant at my direction, and I review and approve it.

Money. Free launch for 30 to 60 days, then introductory prices of $39 (support staff), $49 (opticians) and $59 (optometrists) per 30 day post, then $79 a single post or $199 a month for a clinic pass (up to 5 live posts), with an annual option of $1,990. Prices are before HST. Payments (Stripe) are not built yet. Target revenue is $150,000 a year in 18 to 24 months, on running costs under $10,000 a year.

What the product promises that has legal edges. Every post must show a pay range (no more than $50,000 wide unless the top is over $200,000), say yes or no to "existing vacancy" and to "uses AI to screen candidates", and must not require "Canadian experience". Candidates apply through Kestrel; clinics see an application only after the candidate sends it. Later, Kestrel may list jobs it finds on clinics' own careers pages (labelled "found on the clinic's website, not yet claimed"), expire them after 21 days, and invite the clinic to claim them. That outreach is switched off.

Appendix B. Posting policy, as published

Posting policy

Last updated 30th October 2026

Draft for review. This page is a working draft and has not yet been reviewed by a lawyer. It may change before Kestrel launches.

This policy is for clinics that post jobs on Kestrel. It keeps the board honest and fair for candidates, and it keeps your post in good standing. It is part of our terms of use. We read every post before it goes live.

Who may post

  • Clinics and practices hiring directly for their own team, independent or corporate. Corporate groups may post for their own locations.
  • Suppliers, manufacturers and optical labs hiring directly for their own team, including field based roles such as sales and territory representatives, trainers and clinical educators, and field service technicians.
  • Recruiters, staffing agencies and job brokers may not post, and may not contact candidates through Kestrel.
  • You confirm this when you set up your clinic profile.

What every post must include

  • A pay range for the job, as a yearly salary or an hourly rate. The range should be genuine. Unless the top of the range is over $200,000 a year, it may not be more than $50,000 wide (hourly rates are counted over a standard year of 2,080 hours).
  • Whether this is an existing vacancy, answered honestly with yes or no. Please do not advertise a job that does not exist.
  • Whether you use artificial intelligence to screen candidates, answered honestly with yes or no.
  • The role, the type of work (full time, part time, locum or contract), and the city and province. For a field based role, the city the person is based in and the area they cover.
  • If part of the pay is commission, a car allowance or similar, say so. The pay range is still required, and it should show the base pay.
  • A clear description of the job in your own words.

What is not allowed

  • Discrimination. Posts must not prefer or exclude people because of race, ancestry, colour, ethnic origin, citizenship, place of origin, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, age, marital or family status, disability, or any other ground protected by human rights law. Please do not include wording about age, appearance, accent or family plans. Requirements must relate to the work, such as registration, qualifications and skills.
  • "Canadian experience" requirements. Please do not ask for it. You may ask for the skills and qualifications the job needs.
  • Fees charged to candidates, or any request for money, a deposit or banking details.
  • Misleading posts: wrong pay, wrong location, a different job from the one described, or a job that is already filled.
  • Posts from agencies, including "our client" or "confidential employer" posts.
  • Contact details in the text. Please leave email addresses, phone numbers and web links out of the description. Candidates apply through Kestrel, or through a clinic application page you give us.
  • Anything unlawful, offensive or unrelated to eye care hiring.

How posts are reviewed

  • Each post is read by our team before it is published. We may send it back to you with a note on what to change.
  • A published post stays live for 30 days, then it expires. You can close it sooner, for example when the role is filled.
  • A clinic can have up to 10 open jobs at a time (drafts, in review and live together). Monthly passes include up to 5 live posts.
  • We may take down a post at any time if it breaks this policy, or after a report. We will tell you, and you can fix and resubmit it.

Looking after applicants

  • Please open and answer applications. Candidates can see whether you have viewed their application, and we remind you when applications have waited too long.
  • When a job is filled or closed, update the status of the applicants so no one is left waiting.
  • Use applicants' details only for hiring for the job they applied for, unless they agree to be kept in mind for future roles.

If we remove a post or close an account

Repeated or serious breaches, such as posting as a recruiter, a scam, or discriminatory content, may lead to a suspended account. If you believe we made a mistake, write to hello@eyecarejobs.ca and a person will look at it.

One page. Keep it where you can find it quickly. A plain English summary of Canada's privacy law (PIPEDA), not legal advice. Alberta, British Columbia and Quebec also have their own rules, so ask the lawyer which apply. Read with docs/security.md.

What counts

Any time personal information we hold (names, emails, phone numbers, CVs, cover letters, who applied where) is lost, or seen, taken or shared by someone who should not have it. Examples: a stolen key, a bug that shows one clinic another clinic's applicants, an email sent to the wrong list, a lost laptop with a downloaded spreadsheet, or a service we use telling us they were breached.

What to do

1. In the first hour: stop it getting worse.

  • Change the keys involved, most powerful first (docs/security.md). Sign people out if needed. Take down the page or switch off the feature.
  • Write down the time you found out and who told you.

2. The same day: write it down. Add a line to the log below. Do this even if you are not sure it matters. Do not delete anything that might show what happened (logs, emails, deployments).

3. Within a day or two: decide if it is serious. The law asks whether there is a real risk of significant harm to the people involved. Think about:

  • How sensitive is it? A CV and phone number is personal. A health record or bank detail would be worse. We hold no health or payment details.
  • How likely is it to be misused? Was it taken on purpose by a criminal, or seen briefly by one trusted person who has deleted it? Was it scrambled so it cannot be read?
  • How many people?

If you are not sure, treat it as serious and call the lawyer.

4. If it is serious: tell people, quickly.

  • Report it to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada as soon as you can, using their online breach report form.
  • Tell each person affected directly, by email. See the message below.
  • If the people affected are candidates whose applications a clinic can see (or the reverse), also tell that clinic. Ask the lawyer who is responsible for the information once a clinic has opened an application.
  • Tell anyone else who can help reduce the harm, if that applies.
  • Ask the lawyer whether Alberta, British Columbia or Quebec's regulators also need to hear.

5. Always: keep the record for 24 months, including breaches that did not need reporting.

6. Afterwards: fix the cause, update docs/security.md, and tell the lawyer what changed.

What to say to the people affected

Subject: An important message about your Kestrel information

Hello [first name],

We are writing to tell you about a security problem at Kestrel Eye Care Jobs. On [date], we found that [what happened, in plain words]. This involved your [what information, for example name, email address and CV].

What we have done: [what you did to stop it and fix it].

What you can do: [for example, be careful with unexpected emails that mention Kestrel, and change your password if you used the same one elsewhere].

We are sorry. If you have questions, write to privacy@eyecarejobs.ca and a person will answer. You can also contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.

The Kestrel team

Say what you know and what you do not know yet. Do not guess, blame or promise what you cannot be sure of.
