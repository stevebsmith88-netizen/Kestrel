### 8. Guide: Who does what in an eye care clinic

**Colour:** Chestnut · **Guide:** Live from 18th December · **When:** Monday 21st December · **Location:** Canada · **Time:** 8:00 am Toronto

Caption:
```text
If you have only ever been on the patient side of an eye exam, a clinic can look like a handful of jobs. There are more than most people expect.

Optometrists examine eyes and prescribe. Opticians fit and adjust glasses and, depending on the province, contact lenses. Optometric assistants and technicians run the first tests and keep the day moving. Reception and patient care teams book, greet and handle insurance. Practice managers look after the team and the numbers. Behind them are labs, frame companies and suppliers.
```

### 10. Guide: 4 tips for an eye care resume

**Colour:** Chestnut · **Guide:** Live from 8th January · **When:** Monday 11th January · **Location:** Canada · **Time:** 8:00 am Toronto

Caption:
```text
A resume for an eye care job does not need to be fancy. A clean, honest 1 or 2 pages gets read.

Put your registration status near the top if the job needs it, such as "Registered Optician, Ontario" or "registration in progress". Add 2 or 3 lines on who you are and what you offer. Be specific about the tests you have done, the lenses you have fitted and the systems you use. You do not need a photo, your date of birth or your home address.

Full guide: link in bio.

#EyeCareJobs #Resume #Opticians #OptometricAssistant #JobSearch
```
Alt text:
```text
Kestrel guide: 4 tips for an eye care resume. 1, your registration status, if the job needs it. 2, 2 or 3 lines on who you are. 3, the tests, lenses and systems you know. 4, leave off your photo and date of birth.
```
