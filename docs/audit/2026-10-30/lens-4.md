Reach: read every legal page, email path, consent path, export and deletion path and the job rules from the code at commit 9543c20; rendered the public pages with Playwright (before the shared site stopped) and checked cookies, third party requests and the consent wording; tested the "Canadian experience" check with a script. ## Findings

### 4.1 Terms, Posting policy and Privacy policy have not had a lawyer, and the drafts have gaps
- Severity: High  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: The terms are the contract with every clinic and candidate. They are a good plain English start, but several things a lawyer would expect are missing, and people who sign up by email never actually agree to them.
- Evidence: app/terms/page.tsx:14 and app/posting-policy/page.tsx:14 (`const DRAFT = true`, banner from components/DraftNotice.tsx:5); app/privacy/page.tsx has no DRAFT flag although docs/handoff.md lists it for lawyer review too. Email sign up asks only "I agree to the privacy policy" (components/AuthForms.tsx:134-137, checked in lib/auth-actions.ts:43); only the Google path names the terms (AuthForms.tsx:219). Neither path stores which version was agreed or when (lib/auth-actions.ts:53-60 and 156-158 save no terms field). Clinics do agree at profile set up (components/ClinicProfileForm.tsx:46), but that is not recorded either beyond `direct_hire_confirmed_at`.
- Detail: gaps for the lawyer: (1) the contracting party is just "Kestrel Eye Care Jobs"; say whether it is a corporation or a sole proprietor (a sole proprietor trading under another name must register it under Ontario's Business Names Act, s. 2(2)); (2) the liability cap is "the amount you paid us in the 12 months before", which is $0 for every candidate and for every clinic during the free launch (app/terms/page.tsx:110); (3) no indemnity from clinics for their own posts and their use of applicants' data; (4) no clause on who is responsible for applicants' personal information once a clinic opens it (clinic notes, downloaded CVs); (5) nothing about listings Kestrel finds on clinic websites (seeded listings) or how to have one taken down (only the claim page has a "remove it" mailto, app/jobs/[id]/claim/page.tsx:43 and 108-112); (6) nothing on the AI ad builder (the clinic must check the draft and is responsible for it); (7) "using Kestrel after a change means you accept it" (terms:122) may not bind consumers; (8) no French version or language clause (see 4.6); (9) the refund terms (terms:81-82) are for Stripe, which is not built: fine to keep, but check them before prices switch on. The privacy policy is also out of date: it says accounts and job posting are still to come (app/privacy/page.tsx:167).
- Proposed fix: book a 1 to 2 hour lawyer review of all 3 pages with this list. Then add "I agree to the terms of use" to the email sign up box and save `terms_version` and `terms_accepted_at` on the profile.  | Effort: hours (Claude), plus the lawyer  | Who: Lawyer, then Claude

### 4.2 The privacy policy does not say plainly where data is processed, and misses some services and some data
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Likely
- For Steve: The data is stored in Canada, but every page and form on the site is probably run on Vercel computers in the United States, and the policy only says Vercel "may" handle requests there. It also leaves out Google Workspace, which holds the emails people send to privacy@ and hello@.
- Evidence: vercel.json has only a `crons` entry, no `regions`; Vercel's default function region for a new project is Washington DC (iad1) unless changed in Project Settings, Functions. So server actions such as the CV upload (next.config.ts:5-7, 4 MB body limit for CVs), applications, the export at app/account/export/route.ts and every email build run in the US before the data reaches Supabase in Canada. Policy wording: app/privacy/page.tsx:132-137 ("may handle requests ... on servers outside Canada"). Providers listed at app/privacy/page.tsx:107-114: Supabase, Vercel, Resend, Google sign in, Cloudflare, Anthropic. Not listed: Google Workspace (privacy@, hello@, removal requests with names and roles, app/jobs/[id]/claim/page.tsx:43), and the fact that Supabase's own sign up emails go out through Resend (CLAUDE.md, Current state). Data not described: clinics' private notes about applicants (supabase/migrations/0006_candidates_and_applications.sql:108-113), the "viewed" and status history candidates and clinics see, listing claims (name, work email, role, 0012_claim_listing.sql:7-24), and admin review notes. The Anthropic line (privacy:113) is accurate: lib/ad-builder.ts:54-64 sends only the clinic's own job facts, clinic description and free text brief, never applicant data.
- Detail: PIPEDA allows processing outside Canada, but the Privacy Commissioner's 2009 cross border guidelines expect clear notice that information will be processed in another country (Principle 4.8, openness; Principle 4.1.3, accountability for service providers). "May" understates what probably happens on every request. CLAUDE.md asks for data to stay in Canada "where possible": check whether Vercel offers a Canadian function region on your plan. I could not confirm that from here.
- Proposed fix: Steve checks the Vercel function region (Steve's checks, 1). If a Canadian region is offered, set `"regions"` in vercel.json; if not, change the policy to say every request is processed by Vercel in the United States. Add Google Workspace, the clinic notes, claims and status history to the policy, and keep a short list of providers and their locations in docs/security.md.  | Effort: 1 hour  | Who: Steve (region), Claude (wording), Lawyer (final read)

- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: The download is a good feature, but a candidate who asks "what do you hold about me?" does not get the clinic's private notes about them, their waitlist entry, their email choices or their do not email entry, while the file says it is the information held about them.
- Evidence: app/account/export/route.ts:16-53. ### 4.4 Deleting an account does not quite match the privacy policy, and a clinic deletion wipes candidates' records
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: If a clinic deletes its account, every application candidates sent it disappears from those candidates' own trackers without a word, and Kestrel loses the job post records the clinic may be required to keep. The policy also promises a deletion record that the code never writes.
- Evidence: lib/account-actions.ts:37-63 removes CV files, then `auth.admin.deleteUser`, relying on cascades. Scratch DB foreign keys (kestrel_lens): employers.owner_id to auth.users ON DELETE CASCADE, jobs.employer_id CASCADE, applications.job_id CASCADE, job_reports.job_id CASCADE, listing_claims CASCADE. So a clinic deletion removes its jobs, every application and history to them, and every report about them. The storage removal result is ignored (account-actions.ts:55), so a failed removal leaves CV files with nothing pointing at them. The policy (app/privacy/page.tsx:142-145) promises "a minimal record that we received and carried out your request"; nothing in account-actions.ts or any migration writes one. The waitlist row (keyed by email) is not removed. The candidate warning is clear (components/DeleteAccountForm.tsx:14); candidates who applied to a deleting clinic get no notice.
- Detail: Ontario's Employment Standards Act now requires employers with 25 or more employees to keep every publicly advertised job posting and its application form for 3 years after it comes down (ESA Part III.1 record keeping, in force 1st January 2026; I am confident of the duty, less sure of the section number). That is the clinic's duty, not Kestrel's, but Kestrel is where the posting lives. The clinic export (route.ts:46-52) includes job posts, which helps; it leaves out the application form questions and the extra job fields.
- Proposed fix: on clinic deletion, keep the job rows and the candidates' copies of their applications (detach them from the employer instead of cascading), and tell affected candidates by email; keep job posts for 3 years after they end; check the storage removal result and log failures; write a small `deletion_log` row (date, role, no personal data) or remove that promise from the policy; also delete the waitlist row for the same email, or say that it stays.  | Effort: 1 day  | Who: Claude, Lawyer (how long to keep)

### 4.5 No retention periods are stated or enforced
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: Kestrel keeps everything forever at the moment: old applications, CVs attached to them, unconfirmed waitlist sign ups (which can be a stranger's address), reports with reporters' emails, and claims. The law expects you to decide how long you keep each kind of data and then delete it.
- Evidence: the daily job (app/api/cron/daily/route.ts:45-150) expires jobs, closes out applications and sends emails, and deletes nothing. The policy (app/privacy/page.tsx:139-146) covers only "while you are on the list" and "while your account is open". No migration has a retention or purge function (`grep -in "delete from\|purge\|retention"` over supabase/migrations, lib and app finds nothing). "Kept for future roles" consent (components/ApplyForm.tsx:101-102) has no end date and no way for the candidate to withdraw it in Kestrel (no code updates `keep_details` after applying; `grep -rn keep_details lib app` shows only reads and the insert).
- Detail: PIPEDA Schedule 1, Principle 4.5 (4.5.2 and 4.5.3): set minimum and maximum retention periods and destroy or anonymise what is no longer needed. Quebec's private sector Act, s. 23, says the same. The 3 year Ontario job posting rule (4.4) is a reason to keep job posts, not applications.
- Proposed fix: agree a table with the lawyer, for example: unconfirmed waitlist rows 30 days; applications and their CV copies 2 years after the job ends; "kept for future roles" 12 months unless renewed; reports and claims 2 years; job posts 3 years. Put it in the policy, add a purge step to the daily job, and let candidates turn "kept for future roles" off in their tracker.  | Effort: 1 day  | Who: Lawyer (periods), Claude (code)

### 4.6 Quebec is open everywhere, but the French and Law 25 work has not been done
- Severity: Medium (decide before launch)  | Confidence: Likely
- For Steve: Anyone can already pick Quebec on the waitlist, on a candidate profile, on a job alert and on a job post. The business plan says French and Quebec's privacy law come before marketing in Quebec, but nothing stops Quebec clinics and candidates joining now, and the site, terms and emails are English only.
- Evidence: PROVINCES includes "Quebec" (lib/waitlist.ts:28), used by the waitlist, jobs (lib/jobs.ts:5-7), alerts and candidate profiles; `<html lang="en-CA">` (app/layout.tsx:30) and no French text anywhere (`grep -rni "french\|quebec" app components lib` finds only the province list and the accent check). The plan: business-plan-v7.txt line 32 ("French language version, Phase 3, needed before actively marketing to Quebec clinics") and line 57 ("Quebec's Law 25 adds duties ... before marketing actively in Quebec").
- Detail: what I would expect a Quebec lawyer to raise (confidence: Likely on the duties, Possible on how far they reach an Ontario business with no Quebec office): (1) Law 25, through Quebec's Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector: publish the privacy officer's title and contact details (s. 3.1; the policy gives "privacy officer" and privacy@, which may be enough, but naming the person is safer), publish governance rules (s. 3.2), do a privacy impact assessment before personal information leaves Quebec (s. 17; Vercel, Resend, Cloudflare, Google and Anthropic are in the US), keep an incident register and tell the Commission d'accès à l'information about serious incidents (s. 3.5 and 3.8). (2) The Charter of the French Language as amended by Bill 96: a standard form contract such as the terms of use must be offered in French first (s. 55), and a Quebec employer that publishes a job offer in English must publish it in French at the same time with comparable reach (s. 41). The second is the clinic's duty, but it lands on Kestrel's board. The export is already in a structured format, which suits Law 25's portability right (s. 27).
- Proposed fix: before launch, choose one: (a) keep Quebec out for now (hide it from the province lists, or show "Quebec coming later" and do not accept Quebec posts), or (b) do the Quebec work: French terms and privacy policy first, a privacy impact assessment for the US services, French job posts for Quebec clinics, and the incident register. (a) is about 1 hour.  | Effort: 1 hour for (a); days plus a Quebec lawyer for (b)  | Who: Steve (decision), Lawyer, Claude

### 4.7 Seeded listings would make Kestrel state facts it does not know, and the 21 day rule is not in the code
- Severity: Medium (before any seeding)  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: A listing found on a clinic's website must still show "Existing vacancy: Yes or No" and "Uses AI to screen candidates: Yes or No", because the database insists on them. The clinic never told us either answer, so Kestrel would be publishing a guess under the clinic's name.
- Evidence: the completeness check has no exception for seeded jobs (supabase/migrations/0005_clinics_and_jobs.sql:103-118 requires pay, `is_existing_vacancy` and `uses_ai_screening` for every non draft job). The job page prints both answers for every job (app/jobs/[id]/page.tsx:91-92); the rendered seeded sample job 22222222... showed "Existing vacancy | Yes | Uses AI to screen candidates | No". The claim page promises clinics can "add your pay range if it was missing" (app/jobs/[id]/claim/page.tsx:20) and so does the outreach email (lib/outreach.ts:34), but a live job without a pay range is impossible. Nothing sets a 21 day expiry: the only lifetime is `JOB_LIFETIME_DAYS = 30` (lib/jobs.ts:35) and there is no constraint on `expires_at` for `is_seeded` rows; the claim page simply prints whatever `expires_at` holds (claim/page.tsx:62). Good: there is no logo column at all, so a clinic logo cannot be shown (0005:33-46); the seeded label and link back are in place (app/jobs/[id]/page.tsx:50-52, app/jobs/page.tsx:106); the "remove it" route exists (claim/page.tsx:108-112).
- Detail: the seeding tool is not built, so nothing is wrong on the live site today. When it is built: copying a clinic's ad word for word can infringe its copyright (facts are free, wording is not), and some clinic sites' own terms forbid automated copying. "Own words" can only be a process rule unless the tool rewrites the text.
- Proposed fix: allow the two answers to be empty for seeded jobs and show "Not stated by the clinic" until claimed; seed only jobs whose careers page shows pay; add a check that a seeded job's `expires_at` is at most 21 days after `posted_at`; fix the claim page and outreach copy; have the seeding tool rewrite descriptions and keep the source page and date. Ask the lawyer about copying from clinic sites and stating disclosures for them.  | Effort: 2 to 3 hours  | Who: Claude, Lawyer

### 4.8 The "Canadian experience" check misses common wording and some fields
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: The rule against "Canadian experience" is a selling point and an Ontario law, but the automatic check only catches the exact phrase and 2 close variants. Wording such as "Canadian clinical experience" or "Canadian-experience preferred" gets through unless you spot it in review.
- Evidence: lib/jobs.ts:93. Script $S/lens-4/cdnexp.mjs tested 26 phrases: caught 6 ("Canadian experience required", "canadian work experience", "Experience in Canada", "experience working in Canada", "local experience", "2 years of experience in Canada"); missed 20, including "Canadian clinical experience", "Canadian optometry experience", "Canadian-experience preferred", "Cdn experience", "Canada experience", "experience within Canada", "previous experience in a Canadian clinic", "experience gained in Canada", "Canadian work history", "must have worked in Canada", "Canadian employment experience", "North American experience". Only title and description are checked (lib/jobs.ts:139 and 215); "Also part of the pay" (`pay_extras`), "Area covered" (`territory`) and the clinic "About" text, all shown on the job page (app/jobs/[id]/page.tsx:59-63, 88, 90), are not.
- Detail: Ontario ESA Part III.1, job postings (added by the Working for Workers Four Act, 2024, in force 1st January 2026; I am sure of the rule, not of the exact section number) bars "any requirements related to Canadian experience" in a publicly advertised posting or its application form, for employers with 25 or more employees. Kestrel's own policy bans it for everyone. Since the admin reads every post, this is a safety net, not the only control. Kestrel's own application form asks only for profession, licensed provinces, years of experience and availability (lib/candidate-actions.ts:147-155), which is fine.
- Proposed fix: widen the pattern to "canad\w*|cdn|local|north american" within a few words of "experience|work history|employment", plus "(worked|experience\w*|practi[sc]\w*) (in|within) (canada|a canadian)", allow hyphens; run it over pay extras, area covered and the clinic about text; add the 20 phrases to lib/jobs.test.ts; keep the admin flag for "experience in Ontario" style wording rather than blocking it.  | Effort: 1 hour  | Who: Claude

### 4.9 The waitlist spreadsheet is the only way to send the launch email, and it mixes in people who must not be emailed
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Confirmed
- For Steve: When you send the launch email, you will do it from the downloaded spreadsheet. That file includes people who unsubscribed, people who never confirmed, and addresses on the do not email list, and it lacks what you need to give each person a working unsubscribe link.
- Evidence: app/admin/waitlist.csv/route.ts:20-27 exports every row, with no filter on `unsubscribed_at`, `confirmed_at` or `email_suppressions` (including whole domains), and does not export the row `id` that the unsubscribe link needs (`/unsubscribe/<id>`, app/api/waitlist/route.ts:66). The admin page tells you to "Email the ones marked Confirmed first" (app/admin/waitlist/page.tsx:31). No code sends a launch or news email (every `sendEmail` call is listed under 4.10).
- Detail: CASL s. 6(1) needs consent for each marketing email and s. 11(3) says an unsubscribe must be honoured within 10 business days and after that nothing more may be sent. Emailing one unsubscribed person is a breach. Every marketing email also needs the sender, the mailing address and an unsubscribe link (s. 6(2)).
- Proposed fix: add a "ready to email" download that keeps only confirmed, not unsubscribed, not suppressed rows and includes each person's unsubscribe link; better still, send the launch email from Kestrel itself through `sendEmail` with `unsubscribeUrl`, which already checks the do not email list.  | Effort: 1 to 2 hours  | Who: Claude

### 4.11 Kestrel could help clinics meet their own Ontario duties (45 day interview answer, accommodation notice)
- Severity: Idea  | Confidence: Likely
- For Steve: Ontario clinics with 25 or more staff must tell interviewed candidates the outcome within 45 days, and every Ontario employer must tell applicants that accommodation for disabilities is available. Kestrel could do most of this for them, which is a selling point.
- Evidence: applications can be marked "interview" (0006:73-75), but nothing follows up: the close out only touches applied, viewed and shortlisted (cron:53) and the 14 day reminder only "applied" (cron:73), so an interviewed candidate can wait with no reminder to the clinic. No job page or apply form mentions accommodation (`grep -rni accommodat app components` finds nothing).
- Detail: the 45 day duty comes from the Working for Workers Five Act, 2024 (ESA Part III.1, in force 1st January 2026, employers with 25 or more employees). The accommodation notice is Ontario's Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation, O. Reg. 191/11, s. 22 and 23, which applies to employers. For Kestrel itself, AODA's website rule (O. Reg. 191/11, s. 14, WCAG 2.0 AA) applies only to organisations with 50 or more employees, and compliance reports start at 20, so Kestrel does not need a formal accessibility statement now (a short one is good practice; lens 5 covers the pages themselves). Also: the 2 disclosures are not in the Google for Jobs data (lib/jobs.ts:298-320), and any future AI matching feature would oblige clinics that use it to answer "Yes" to the AI question.
- Proposed fix: send the clinic a reminder 30 days after an "interview" status; add a line on Ontario job pages and the apply form: "Need an accommodation to apply or interview? Say so in your cover letter and the clinic will work with you."; consider adding the 2 disclosures as a closing line of the visible description so they travel with the Google listing.  | Effort: 2 hours  | Who: Claude, Lawyer (wording)

### 4.13 "Reply to any email to unsubscribe" may go nowhere until the reply address is set
- Severity: Medium  | Confidence: Possible (depends on a Vercel setting)
- For Steve: The privacy policy tells people they can stop emails by replying to any Kestrel email, and the outreach email says "just reply" to have a listing taken down. Unless the reply address is set in Vercel, those replies go to the sending address, which is not an inbox, so a request to stop could be lost.
- Evidence: app/privacy/page.tsx:93-95 ("withdraw your consent at any time by emailing privacy@eyecarejobs.ca or replying to any email from us. We will act on your request within 10 business days"); lib/outreach.ts:34 ("Just reply to this email"). lib/email.ts:7 and 26 send from `hello@mail.eyecarejobs.ca` and add `reply_to` only if `EMAIL_REPLY_TO` is set; docs/email-addresses.md:16 says that from address is "A sending address, not an inbox"; setting `EMAIL_REPLY_TO` is still open (docs/handoff.md:63, docs/launch-checklist.md:55). - Detail: CASL s. 11(1) lets the unsubscribe work by reply, and s. 11(3) gives 10 business days to act. A promised channel that silently drops requests is the kind of thing that turns a complaint into a finding against you. PIPEDA Principle 4.3.8 covers withdrawing consent.
- Proposed fix: set `EMAIL_REPLY_TO=hello@eyecarejobs.ca` in Vercel (Production and Preview), confirm hello@ and privacy@ reach a person, and adjust the policy sentence about consent and alerts.  | Effort: minutes  | Who: Steve, then Claude (wording)
