# Legal on a bootstrap budget

12th November 2026. This is a plan for keeping legal costs low, not legal advice. Prices are rough guides; check them when you book.

## The idea in 1 line

Pay a lawyer only for what no template or government guide can answer for you, and design the product so that most of the other legal questions never come up.

## Pay for only 2 things

**1. Your employment agreement and the disclosure letter. Do this first. It is the one real risk.**
- Start free: the Law Society of Ontario's Referral Service gives a free consultation of up to 30 minutes with a lawyer. Ask for an employment lawyer, bring the agreement and a 1 page summary of Kestrel, and ask: "Does sections 7 and 9 restrict this, and what should my disclosure say?"
- If more is needed, ask that lawyer for a flat fee to review the agreement and the disclosure letter. Expect a few hundred dollars to about $1,000.
- This is outside a paralegal's licence in Ontario, so it needs a lawyer.

**2. One flat fee review of the Terms, Privacy policy and Posting policy, before you take any money.**
- Claude prepares a short pack: the 3 documents, plus no more than 5 questions written into them (who is responsible for an applicant's information once a clinic opens it; the liability clause; the operator's name; the retention schedule; the fake job policy).
- Book it through a flat fee service such as Goodlawyer, or with the lawyer from step 1. Expect a few hundred dollars to about $1,000.
- Until then, the free launch runs on your own well kept drafts, which is normal for a business taking no payments.

## Not a lawyer at all

- **Incorporating:** doable yourself online through the Ontario or federal registry (government fees of a few hundred dollars), or through an online incorporation service. Talk to an accountant first about HST, structure and timing. Before paid launch is a sensible point.
- **HST, Stripe Tax:** an accountant.
- **Trademark, insurance:** later, from revenue.

## What happens to the 45 question pack

Keep it as background for yourself. Do not pay anyone to read it. The lawyer for step 1 needs the employment section only; the reviewer for step 2 needs the 3 policies and the 5 questions.
