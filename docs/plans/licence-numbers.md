# Licence numbers on candidate profiles: research and plan (19th November 2026)

Decided with Steve (19th November 2026): ask for the licence number on the candidate profile, not at sign up. It is self reported and shown to the clinic inside an application, labelled "entered by the candidate, not checked by Kestrel". Kestrel never looks a number up itself (no automated collection from college registers). Next to the number is a small button with the external link icon (an arrow leaving a box) that opens the college's public register in a new tab, so the clinic can check it. Required to apply for optometrist jobs, and for optician jobs where the province regulates opticians. Research below, 2 provinces first (Ontario and BC), the rest to follow.

## British Columbia (confirmed from the regulator's own site)

- Since 28th June 2024 optometrists and opticians are regulated by the **College of Health and Care Professionals of BC (CHCPBC)**, which replaced the College of Opticians of BC and the College of Optometrists of BC (and others). Old college names and links are out of date.
- Public registry (labelled "View Public Registry" on chcpbc.org): https://chcpbc.alinityapp.com/client/publicdirectory . It shows licence status, certifications, and any limits or conditions. CHCPBC's licence verification page says a person can be searched by name or licence number (that page's address returned an error when fetched, so check the wording by hand).
- Optician categories on CHCPBC's application form: **Optician/contact lens fitter**; **Optician with refracting certification**; **Optician/contact lens fitter with refracting certification**. CHCPBC says an optician needs additional training and an additional licence to use the title "contact lens fitter", and the Opticians Regulation restricts contact lens fitting to contact lens fitters or people supervised by one. So in BC, "contact lens fitter" is a real licence level, and "optician only" is possible.
- Sources: chcpbc.org (home page, Opticianry application form), BC Opticians Regulation (gov.bc.ca).

## Ontario

- Optometrists: **College of Optometrists of Ontario**, register called "Find an Optometrist", linked from collegeoptom.on.ca: https://members.collegeoptom.on.ca/COO/PublicDirectory/Public_Directory_Member/Public_Register/PublicRegisterMember.aspx
- Opticians: **College of Opticians of Ontario**, public register: https://members.collegeofopticians.ca/Public-Register
- Opticians' registration classes (O. Reg. 869/93 under the Opticianry Act, 1991): registered optician, registered student optician, registered intern optician, emergency optician. A registered optician may dispense contact lenses, eye glasses and subnormal vision devices, on the prescription of an optometrist or physician, subject to conditions on their certificate. There is no separate "contact lens fitter only" class in Ontario in what I found, so the dual licence question does not arise in the same way as in BC. A student optician dispenses only under a registered optician.
- **College of Optometrists of Ontario register, as seen by Steve (screenshots, 19th November 2026).** Confirmed. No real person's details are copied here.
  - **Search fields:** Last Name, Registration #, City, Postal Code, Registration Status (Any), Gender, Language of Service, Accessibility, and a Find button. The page says any 1 field or any combination can be used, and shows detailed information about every member, including registration status, any terms, conditions or limitations, and disciplinary information where there is some. So a clinic can type a registration number and find the person.
  - **Results list:** a table with Member Name, Status, Class, Practice Location (clinic name, address, phone) and an Additional Details link where there is a concern or finding. The whole register is listed (4078 items in 204 pages when searched with no filters). Statuses seen: Member Practising, Member Non-Practising, Resigned. Classes seen: General practising, General inactive. The registration number is not in the list.
  - **A person's page:** Registration # (4 digits with a leading zero, for example 0717), Initial Registration Date, Registration Class, Registration Status, Former Surname, Languages of Practice, Gender, Education (school and country), Year of Graduation, Completed Equivalency, and Authorized to prescribe drugs. Tabs below: Registration History (each status with an effective date, so a past suspension shows), Practice Locations, Professional Corporation, Concerns and Findings.
  - **What it means for us:** the number is searchable, so a clinic can check it in under a minute. A number alone is not proof of good standing: the clinic should check the Registration Status says Member Practising, and that the name (or a former surname) matches. The register page cannot be opened with a number filled in, so our button opens the register and shows the number beside it to copy. Keep the number as text, never a number, so the leading zero is kept.
- **College of Opticians of Ontario register, as seen by Steve (screenshots, 19th November 2026).** Confirmed. No real person's details are copied here.
  - **Search fields:** Last Name, First Name Contains, Informal Name Contains, Registration Number, Registration Class, Registration Status, Contact Lens Mentor, Area(s) of Service, Language(s) of service, Practice Name, City or Town, Postal Code, and a Find button. Any combination can be used, so a clinic can search by registration number alone.
  - **Results list:** Registrant, Status, Class, Practice Location (practice name and address) and a View Member Details link. Statuses seen: Entitled to Practise, Entitled to Practise under Supervision, Intern Status Expired. Classes seen: Registered Optician, Intern. In the screenshots the Registrant column showed a first name followed by a dash, with no surname. The registration number is not in the list.
  - **A person's page:** Registration Number (4 digits, for example 5841), Date of Initial Registration, Date of Registration as Registered Optician, Name used in practice, Categories of Patients, Registrant Type, Languages of Care, Registration Status, and **Areas of Practice** (the example showed Contact Lenses, Eye Glasses, Safety Glasses). Folded sections below: Registration History, Practice Locations, Professional Corporation, and Concerns, Practice Restrictions or Findings.
  - **What it means for us:** the register shows the person's areas of practice, so whether an Ontario optician fits contact lenses, glasses or both is something a clinic can check there. That answers most of the "dual licence or contact lens only" question for Ontario without a new licence level on our side: a candidate says what they do (glasses, contact lenses, both) and the clinic can confirm it on the register. Interns and registered opticians use the same number field, and the clinic sees the status ("Entitled to Practise" is the one to look for; "under Supervision" is an intern).
- **BC public registry (CHCPBC), as seen by Steve (screenshots, 19th November 2026).** Confirmed for optometry. No real person's details are copied here.
  - **Search fields:** First name, Last name, Registration # (called Licensee # elsewhere on the page), and an optional profession. At least 1 of the 3 fields is needed. The search returns at most 25 records at a time. **In June 2026 CHCPBC issued new licensee numbers to everyone, and the old numbers cannot be used to search.** One registry covers audiology, dietetics, hearing instrument dispensing, occupational therapy, opticianry, optometry, physical therapy, psychology and speech-language pathology. It also lists former licensees whose licence was cancelled within the last 5 years, and current practice restrictions.
  - **Results list:** Name with a **masked licensee number** (only the last 2 digits, for example **30), Licence Class with the profession (for example Full, Optometry), Licence Status (Authorized to Practise, or Not Authorized to Practise for a former licensee), a Details button, and a warning triangle beside some names.
  - **A person's page:** "Status as of" with the date and time, full name, Licensee # (masked, last 2 digits), Name they go by, Licence Class (an optometrist showed "DO - Full") with the status and Effective and Expiry dates, Employment Information (practice names, phone, city), **Certifications** (None in the optometry example), Current Practice Restrictions, Regulatory Actions, and a Licensure History button (last 5 years).
  - **What it means for us:** the register hides most of the number, so a clinic cannot read the number off the page. It can type the full number we show it into the search and check that the name, profession, status and last 2 digits match. That is the check we describe. Candidates who still quote a number from before June 2026 will find nothing, so the form must say "your licensee number issued in June 2026 or later". Expiry dates are shown, which is useful (a licence expiring soon). Whether a contact lens fitter licence shows under Certifications for an optician is not yet seen.
- **Still not confirmed:** a BC optician's person page, to see where the contact lens fitter licence and refracting certification show.

## What this means for the build

- The licence number field needs a province, since the college and the register differ. Ontario and BC have 1 register link per profession. BC has 1 shared registry for both.
- A licence level choice ("contact lens fitter only", "optician with contact lens fitting") is needed for BC. Ontario needs none beyond registered optician (students and interns are a separate question for the apply rule).
- Other provinces still to research. Some may not regulate opticians at all, in which case the field is not shown.
- Open: the exact label each college uses for the number (registration number, licence number, member number), so the form uses the right word.

## What this means for the build (revised)

- **The field is per profession and per province, and it is only shown where there is something to check.** Optometrists: every province and territory has a register. Opticians: every province except Yukon, the Northwest Territories and Nunavut, where nobody can be asked for a number because there is no regulator.
- **Contact lens fitting as a separate level exists, or may exist, in BC (a licence level), Nova Scotia (a specialty register) and Newfoundland and Labrador (a special register), and perhaps PEI (a special class).** In Ontario it shows as an area of practice, and in Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba nothing separate was seen. So the profile question should be "Which of these do you do?" (glasses, contact lenses, both) for all opticians, and a clinic reads the register for the rest, instead of us building a licence level for every province.
- **Alberta and BC use the same registry software (Alinity).** BC masks most of the number. Alberta's behaviour is not yet seen.
- **Provinces where the register is only a directory, not a register with numbers (Saskatchewan optometrists so far)** need the button to say "Look this person up" rather than "Check this number".
- **To finish:** a person's page from Alberta (both colleges), Manitoba opticians, and a check of the register links for Saskatchewan opticians, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador and PEI. Quebec later.

## What the contact lens research now says

Contact lens practice is its own registration, level or advanced practice in **BC, Alberta, Manitoba, Nova Scotia (statute) and Newfoundland and Labrador (older text)**, and an area of practice in **Ontario**. So the profile asks every optician "What are you registered to do?" with 2 ticks (Eyeglasses, Contact lenses), and each clinic can check it on the register. I earlier wrote that Alberta and Manitoba showed nothing separate. That is corrected above.

## Register button design (per register)

Each register is described once, in 1 list in the code, with: its name, address, whether it can be searched by number, and whether the number is shown on a person's page. The button's wording and help line come from that:
- **Search by number works (Ontario both colleges, BC):** "Check this number on the register." Help line: "Search for this number, then check the name and status match."
- **Search by name only, number shown on the page (Alberta opticians):** "Look this person up on the register." Help line: "Search the last name, then check the registration number and status match."
- **Name only, number not seen (Manitoba opticians):** "Look this person up on the register." Help line: "Search the surname, then check the status and that they are registered."
- **Register not confirmed yet:** a link to the regulator's own website, and "Check with the college" wording, until the register is seen.
